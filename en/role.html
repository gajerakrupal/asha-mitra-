badge1. Who is an ASHA

personA link between people and the health system

ASHA means Accredited Social Health Activist. You are a woman from the village, chosen by the village, usually one ASHA for about 1000 people. You connect families with health services.

lightbulb
Remember

You are the first person families come to for health help. Your trust is your strength.

checklistYour three roles

1. Facilitator — you bring people to services like ANC, immunization, delivery and screening. 2. Service provider — you give first aid, ORS, zinc, IFA, contraceptives, pregnancy test kits and malaria tests. 3. Activist — you make the village aware of its health rights.

lightbulb
Remember

Always tell families which services are free.

medicationYour drug kit

Your kit has: ORS, zinc, paracetamol, IFA tablets, albendazole, condoms, oral pills, pregnancy test kit, cotton, bandage, antiseptic, and malaria RDT and medicines where needed. Check expiry dates and refill at the monthly PHC meeting.

lightbulb
Remember

Keep the kit clean, dry and away from children.

groups2. VHSNC, VHSND and meetings

groupsVillage Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Committee

The VHSNC plans health, water, sanitation and nutrition work for the village. It meets every month. The ASHA is the member-secretary or convenor. The committee has an untied fund that can be used for village needs, such as cleaning drives or emergency help for poor families.

lightbulb
Remember

Keep minutes of every meeting and a record of how the fund is spent.

calendar_monthVHSND — village health day

Village Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Day is held every month at the Anganwadi with the ANM and Anganwadi worker. Services: ANC check-ups, immunization, weighing children, nutrition counselling, family planning and NCD screening.

lightbulb
Remember

Bring the due list. Make home visits the day before to remind families.

local_hospitalMonthly meeting at PHC

Attend the monthly ASHA meeting at the PHC or health centre. Here you share your work, learn new things, solve problems, refill your drug kit and submit claims for incentives.

lightbulb
Remember

Write down your questions during the month and ask them at the meeting.

menu_book3. Records and incentives

menu_bookYour registers

Keep your ASHA diary or register up to date: household list, eligible couples, pregnant women, births and deaths, immunization due list, children under 5, fever and TB cases, and people aged 30+ with CBAC.

lightbulb
Remember

Write the same day. Memory fades, records do not.

fact_checkCorrect records save lives

Your records help the ANM and doctor know who needs care. Correct numbers help plan vaccines, medicines and camps. Never write something that did not happen.

lightbulb
Remember

Check your list with the ANM and Anganwadi worker every month.

workspace_premiumIncentives

You get a fixed monthly amount for routine work and extra incentives for specific tasks, such as institutional delivery, full immunization, TB treatment support and family planning. Payment comes directly to your bank account.

lightbulb
Remember

Keep copies of your claim forms. Ask your ASHA facilitator if payment is late.

campaign4. Home visits and counselling

homeWhich homes first

Visit these homes first: pregnant women, newborns and mothers after delivery, children under 2 years, sick people, TB patients, and bedridden or elderly people. Plan your visits every week.

lightbulb
Remember

Carry your register, MCP cards and drug kit on every visit.

campaignGood counselling

Greet with respect. Ask questions and listen fully. Use simple words and pictures. Give one or two main messages at a time. Ask them to repeat the message back to check understanding. Agree on the next step.

lightbulb
Remember

Listen more than you speak.

family_restroomInclude the family

Many decisions are made by the husband or mother-in-law. Include them when talking about delivery, family planning or nutrition. Respect privacy — some topics should be discussed only with the woman.

lightbulb
Remember

Never share a person's health problem with others in the village.

4. Which is the best way to check that a mother understood your advice?

  2. Ask her to repeat the message in her own words
  3. Give her a paper
  4. Say it again louder

Answer: Ask her to repeat the message in her own words

Asking her to repeat it back (teach-back) shows what she really understood.

Write the same day so the record is correct.
