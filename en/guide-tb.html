Tuberculosis (TB): finding, testing and supporting patients

personPrepared by Dr. Krupal Gajera, resident doctor in Community Medicine · Last updated: 6 November 2026

The signs of TB, how to help a person get tested for free, how to support six months of treatment, side effects to watch for, and protecting the family.

TB is an infectious disease that usually affects the lungs and spreads through the air when a person with TB coughs or sneezes. India has the largest number of TB patients in the world, but TB is fully curable. Testing and treatment are free in government facilities. As an ASHA, you help find people with TB early, get them tested, and support them until they finish treatment.

Who may have TB

Suspect TB (presumptive TB) in anyone who has any one of these signs:

  • Cough for 2 weeks or more
  • Fever, especially in the evening
  • Weight loss or loss of appetite
  • Night sweats
  • Blood in the sputum
  • Swelling of neck glands, or other long-lasting illness that does not improve

Pay extra attention to family members of TB patients, people with diabetes or HIV, smokers, people who drink alcohol, malnourished people, the elderly, and people who work in dusty places such as mines or stone quarries.

Help with testing

  1. Explain that testing is free and that TB can be cured completely.
  2. Help the person give a sputum sample at the health centre, or collect it if you are trained to.
  3. The sample should come from deep in the chest after a strong cough, ideally early in the morning, collected in the open air away from others.
  4. Sputum is tested by microscopy or NAAT (a machine test). A chest X-ray may also be done.
  5. Follow up the result and make sure treatment starts quickly if TB is found.

Treatment: at least six months, without breaks

  • Treatment lasts at least six months and is free.
  • Medicines must be taken every day. The patient must not stop when feeling better.
  • Stopping early or missing doses can cause drug-resistant TB, which is much harder to treat.
  • You may be the patient's treatment supporter. Check that medicines are taken daily and record it as instructed (for example in Ni-kshay).
lightbulb
Remember

Visit regularly, ask how the patient feels, and count the remaining tablets. Encourage the family to support the patient instead of keeping away.

Side effects to watch for

Orange or red colour of urine is common with TB medicines and is not harmful. Tell the patient to see the doctor quickly if there is:

  • yellow eyes or skin, or severe vomiting and loss of appetite
  • skin rash or itching
  • pain or swelling in the joints
  • tingling or numbness in the hands and feet
  • problems with vision or hearing
warning

Yellow eyes during TB treatment can be a sign of liver damage. The patient should see the doctor the same day.

Support for patients and families

  • Every TB patient gets monthly nutrition support under Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana, paid to the bank account. Help link the bank account details in Ni-kshay.
  • Good food helps recovery. Encourage pulses, eggs, milk, green vegetables and fruits.
  • Screen all family members and close contacts for TB signs. Some of them, especially young children, may be given TB preventive treatment.
  • Patients should cover the mouth when coughing, not spit in the open, and keep windows open for fresh air.
  • Fight stigma: TB is not a curse and patients on treatment soon stop spreading it.

Common questions

The patient feels well after two months. Can treatment stop?

No. Feeling better does not mean the TB is gone. The full course must be completed to cure TB and prevent drug-resistant TB.

Do family members need tests if they have no cough?

Family members should be checked for TB signs, and young children and some adults may need preventive treatment even without symptoms. The doctor will decide.

Can a TB patient keep working and living at home?

Yes. After treatment starts, most patients can stay at home and continue normal life, following cough hygiene.

Sources: National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP) guidelines; Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana; NHM ASHA training modules.
