Fever in the village: malaria and dengue, what the ASHA should do

personPrepared by Dr. Krupal Gajera, resident doctor in Community Medicine · Last updated: 6 November 2026

How to test every fever for malaria, give complete treatment, recognise dengue warning signs, give safe home care, and stop mosquitoes from breeding.

Fever is the most common illness you will see. In many areas, malaria and dengue cause a large share of fevers, especially in and after the monsoon. Both can kill, but early testing, complete treatment and quick referral of warning signs save lives. Both also spread by mosquitoes, so prevention in every household is part of your work.

Malaria: test every fever

  • Malaria spreads by the bite of the female Anopheles mosquito, which bites at night.
  • Signs: fever with chills and shivering, sweating, headache and body ache. The fever may come on alternate days.
  • In a malaria area, test every person with fever using a Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT), within 24 hours of the start of fever. Also make a blood slide if you have been trained to.
  • Record every fever case and test result in the fever register.
  • Check the expiry date of RDT kits and store them as instructed.

Malaria: give the complete treatment

If the RDT is positive, give treatment according to the age-wise drug chart in your kit and your training:

  • P. vivax malaria: chloroquine for 3 days and primaquine for 14 days.
  • P. falciparum malaria: ACT (artesunate combination therapy) and a single dose of primaquine.
  • The full course must be completed, even if the fever goes away after one or two days.
  • Do not give primaquine to pregnant women or infants. Refer every pregnant woman with malaria to the Medical Officer.
lightbulb
Remember

Give the first dose in front of you. Visit again on day 3 to check that the fever has gone and the medicines are being taken.

warning

Severe malaria can kill within hours. Refer at once if the person is unconscious or very drowsy, has fits, vomits repeatedly, cannot eat or drink, is very pale, has yellow eyes, dark urine or very little urine.

Dengue and chikungunya

  • Spread by the Aedes mosquito, which has white stripes, bites in the daytime and breeds in clean stored water.
  • Dengue: sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, body and joint pain, and sometimes a rash.
  • Chikungunya: fever with severe joint pain that may last for weeks.
  • Send people with these signs to the health centre for the dengue test.

Safe home care for dengue

  • Plenty of fluids: ORS, water, coconut water, buttermilk, soups and juice.
  • Rest, and keep the patient under a mosquito net so that mosquitoes do not spread it to others.
  • For fever and pain, give only paracetamol in the correct dose.
  • Do not give aspirin, ibuprofen or other pain killers, and do not give injections. They can cause bleeding.

Dengue warning signs: refer at once

Warning signs usually appear between day 3 and day 7, often just when the fever is going down. A falling fever does not always mean the patient is safe. Refer at once if there is:

  • severe pain in the belly, or repeated vomiting
  • bleeding from the gums or nose, blood in vomit, or black stools
  • cold, clammy skin, restlessness or drowsiness
  • passing very little urine, or breathlessness

Stop mosquitoes from breeding

  1. Once a week, empty, scrub and dry coolers, tanks, pots and flower vases (Dry Day).
  2. Cover all water containers with tight lids.
  3. Throw away old tyres, coconut shells and broken pots, or keep them upside down.
  4. Everyone, especially pregnant women and children, should sleep under a long-lasting insecticidal net (LLIN) every night.
  5. Allow indoor residual spraying and do not plaster or paint the walls afterwards.
  6. Larva-eating fish (Gambusia or guppy) can be put in ponds, wells and tanks.

Go house to house with the family and show them the larvae (wrigglers) in stored water. Seeing them is the best way to convince people. Inform your ANM about new breeding places and clusters of fever cases.

Common questions

The RDT is negative but the fever continues. What should I do?

Refer the person to the health centre. Fever can have many causes, and a repeat test or other tests may be needed.

Is papaya leaf juice a treatment for dengue?

There is no proven treatment that raises platelets at home. Give fluids and paracetamol, watch for warning signs, and refer early.

Can a person get malaria again after treatment?

Yes. A new mosquito bite can cause a new infection. Nets and removing breeding places protect the family.

Sources: National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control (NCVBDC) guidelines for malaria and dengue; NHM ASHA training modules.
