Small and preterm babies: Kangaroo Mother Care and home care

personPrepared by Dr. Krupal Gajera, resident doctor in Community Medicine · Last updated: 6 November 2026

How to recognise a low birth weight or preterm baby, how to give Kangaroo Mother Care, feeding a small baby, extra home visits and the danger signs that need referral.

A baby who weighs less than 2.5 kg at birth is called low birth weight. A baby born before 37 weeks of pregnancy is called preterm. These small babies lose heat quickly, find it hard to suck, and catch infections easily. Most newborn deaths in India happen in small babies, yet most of them can be saved with three simple things: warmth, breast milk and early care when they fall sick. You can make sure every small baby in your village gets them.

Find small babies early

  • Weigh every newborn at your first home visit with the HBNC weighing scale, and check the weight written on the discharge card.
  • A baby below 2.5 kg is low birth weight. A baby below 1.8 kg, or one who cannot suck, needs care in a hospital (SNCU or NBSU).
  • Ask whether the baby was born early. A preterm baby may need extra care even if the weight is close to 2.5 kg.
  • Small babies need more home visits than other babies. Plan them with your ANM.

Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC)

In Kangaroo Mother Care the baby is kept skin-to-skin on the mother's chest, like a baby kangaroo in its mother's pouch. The mother's body keeps the baby warm, the baby breastfeeds more often, gains weight better and gets fewer infections.

  1. The baby wears only a cap, socks and a nappy.
  2. The mother sits or reclines comfortably. The baby is placed upright between her breasts, chest to chest, with the head turned to one side and slightly tilted up so that breathing is easy.
  3. Cover the baby's back with the mother's blouse, a shawl or a KMC wrap, and tie it so the baby is supported.
  4. Keep the baby in this position for as many hours as possible in the day and night. Short sessions of less than an hour do not help much.
  5. The father, grandmother or another family member can give KMC when the mother needs rest.
  6. Continue until the baby is about 2.5 kg or starts wriggling out because it is warm enough.
lightbulb
Remember

Show the family how to do KMC with the baby in front of you, and come back the next day to check. Praise the mother and father for every hour they give.

Feeding a small baby

  • Breastfeed every 2 hours, day and night, even if the baby does not cry for feeds. Wake the baby gently if needed.
  • If the baby cannot suck well, teach the mother to express breast milk by hand into a clean cup and give it with a cup, spoon or paladai.
  • Give only breast milk. Do not give water, honey, ghutti, animal milk or formula unless a doctor advises it.
  • Medicines or supplements prescribed at the hospital should be continued exactly as written.

Warmth and hygiene

  • Keep the room warm and free of draughts. Close windows at night.
  • Do not bathe a small baby. Clean it gently with a warm, wet cloth and dry it at once.
  • Everyone must wash hands with soap before touching the baby. Keep sick people away from the baby.
  • Keep the cord clean and dry and apply nothing on it.

Danger signs: refer at once

warning

Small babies get sick quickly. Do not wait for the next visit if you see any of these signs. Arrange transport (108/102) to a facility with an SNCU or NBSU.

  • Not able to suck or feed, or feeding much less than before
  • Cold to touch even after KMC, or very hot
  • Fast breathing (60 or more per minute), chest in-drawing, or pauses in breathing
  • Very sleepy, limp, or fits
  • Yellow palms and soles
  • Weight is going down instead of up
  • Red or pus-filled cord, or many skin boils

Follow-up after the hospital

Babies discharged from an SNCU need regular follow-up visits at the hospital as written on the discharge card, and extra home visits from you. Check the dates, remind the family, and help arrange transport. Record the weight at every visit so that the ANM and doctor can see whether the baby is growing well.

Common questions

Is KMC only for very small babies?

KMC helps every small or preterm baby and is safe for all babies. It is most important for babies below 2.5 kg.

The mother is tired. Can someone else do KMC?

Yes. The father, grandmother or any healthy adult in the family can give KMC while the mother rests.

Can the baby sleep during KMC?

Yes. The baby can sleep in the KMC position. The mother can sleep reclining, with the baby securely tied to her chest.
